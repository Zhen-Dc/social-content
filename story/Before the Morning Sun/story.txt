Summary:
Vincent is a poor young man from Kent who has been caught in a brutal reversal of power after Wazobian soldiers attack and enslave people from his world. He is placed among other captives and assigned work he barely understands, but his real concern is not himself. Hidden close to him is Horatia, his baby sister, the only family he has left after their mother dies.

The story focuses on Vincent trying to survive a night in cramped captivity while protecting Horatia. He remembers their mother, the poverty they lived through, the day everything collapsed, and the promise he feels toward the child. Horatia becomes hungry and begins crying, which puts both of them in danger because any noise could expose her to the guards and the other captives. Vincent tries to calm her with food, affection, and memories of their mother's lullaby.

The emotional center is Vincent's impossible responsibility: he is still young, exhausted, hungry, and powerless, but he refuses to abandon his sister. The tension comes from whether love is enough to keep them alive before morning arrives.
