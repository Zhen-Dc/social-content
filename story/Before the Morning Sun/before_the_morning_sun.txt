The night seemed to stretch for hours as Vincent turned and rolled around the messy floor, his back aching from sharing space with other slaves of his category. He adjusted the dark suit that was "dirtied and torn," having borrowed it a day before the Wazobian attack. Lying in their cramped room, he hoped his bedmate wouldn't stir.

He sat up and gazed at the crowded room, needing sleep, but thoughts kept him awake: visions of his father, who had been described as a "drunken bastard plus a ravenous rake," leaving him with Maureen Lisa Silverton. She had raised him in Kent, showing care despite their poverty. A tear fell as he remembered her cold skin and empty eyes when she died, leaving him the responsibility for his little sister, Horatia. He gazed at her baby form, whispering to himself, "My poor Horatia." Her existence was the only thing keeping him sane.

Despite having spent time doing menial work just to keep her fed, he often worried about providing enough. As exhaustion hit, he finally slept until Horatia stirred. Frantically trying to quiet her crying with stale milk from his mother, Vincent struggled: "Shush Horatia, please…" He tried soothing her by whistling a lullaby that reminded him of his mother. When the cries started up again, alarming nearby men, he panicked, praying no one saw them.

When the commotion began and people started searching for the baby, it was his floor mate who finally spoke, pointing at him: “You with a babe?” Vincent explained quietly, "She is my babe sister, I could not let her go." The man warned him that the situation was dangerous; "you can’t take care of this child." Pressed by questioning, he stated simply, "I can't let her go." To ease the tension, someone in the crowd shouted for food. Vincent embraced his sister, assuring her: "Sssh, baby princess, food is coming." When asked about Horatia, he replied that she was "A year and four months" old and her mother was "Dead." A chunk of stale bread was given to him, causing him to weep openly in relief.

The next morning, Vincent was jolted awake by his floor mate, Harvey, who reminded him how difficult he was to wake. After learning that they were still safe from the whip bell, he thanked Harvey and offered his name: "I am Vincent." At the market, they found themselves forced into lines before a large room titled "MARKET ARCADE: Slave Trading." As soon as they entered, the noise was overwhelming. They stood on the platform while men poked and scrutinized them. One man contemptuously remarked, “Look at this one... He is as thin as twig.”

He felt faint under the gaze of the crowd. Thinking only of Horatia, he closed his eyes, imagining a world where she was safe. Suddenly, an unfamiliar voice called out: "Vincent?” A soldier dragged him away. Upon realizing he had been bought, Vincent sighed with relief, thinking that nothing was left but to wonder if his master would be kind.

* (Scene shift) *

Akande arrived at the auction feeling deep disgust; this whole system was too much. He drank palm wine and wished for a peaceful walk under the stars. His mother scolded him gently, reminding him that "if anyone here suspects that you are not in agreement with the slavery system, you could be killed." To avoid suspicion from spies reporting back to Wazobia, he felt pressured to buy something.

He surveyed the line of slaves until his gaze landed on a wiry boy at the end. He found Mr. Olawale and inquired about "that last set of slaves." After exchanging some local phrases, Akande successfully bought the boy: “Ehm…Vincent, yes Vincent Silverton.” Olawale agreed to terms after much bidding, eventually adding an extra 200 naira for the special treatment.

Akande finally found a quiet spot with two wooden benches and began sipping his third cup of palm wine. "Evening, My Lord," interrupted a voice. Akande lifted his head and saw the boy he had bought. He felt a peculiar pull of emotion toward him. He was small, delicate, yet possessed an intense gaze.

After assessing Vincent, Akande smiled, then dropped his pretense: “I am the Sarkin Yaki assigned to this colony.” When pressed about names, they established that since calling his name is dangerous, Vincent could call him "Valor." Finally, with a knowing look, Akande extended an invitation: “Come, sit. I trust they have no need of you again.”
