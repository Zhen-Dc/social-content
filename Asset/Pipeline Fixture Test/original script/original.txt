Amara stood at the small kitchen window and watched the rain gather along the zinc roof. Her brother Kelechi had promised to return before evening, but the road outside their compound was already turning into red mud. Their mother, Ngozi, kept stirring the soup as if the spoon could hide her fear. When a neighbor knocked and whispered that Kelechi had been seen near the old storehouse, Amara felt her chest tighten. She took her scarf, stepped into the rain, and followed the narrow path behind the market. The storehouse door was open. Inside, Kelechi was sitting on a broken crate, shaking but alive. He had found the missing school money hidden under a sack of cassava. Amara hugged him, then turned toward the doorway where their uncle stood silently, caught before he could run.
