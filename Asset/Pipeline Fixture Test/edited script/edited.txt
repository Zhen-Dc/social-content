I stood by our small kitchen window, watching rain drum against the zinc roof while my fear grew heavier with every minute. My brother had promised he would return before evening, but the road outside our compound was already sinking into red mud. My mother kept stirring the soup, pretending she was calm, though I could see panic in her hands. Then a neighbor came to the door and whispered that my brother had been seen near the old storehouse. I grabbed my scarf and ran into the rain. When I reached the storehouse, the door was open. My brother was inside, alive and trembling, with the missing school money under a sack of cassava. Then I saw our uncle in the doorway, silent and trapped.
