My mother, Hajia Amina, sat on the edge of the couch with her hands folded so tightly her knuckles looked pale. She did not look at me. My father sat beside her with his back straight and his face heavy, like someone had placed a stone inside his chest.

The room felt wrong before anyone spoke.

"I am here, Baba," I said.

He looked at me for a long moment. "Zahra, you are growing into a young woman now."

My throat tightened. Adults only began like that when they had already made a decision for you.

"Yes, Baba."

He sighed, and for the first time that night I saw that he was not only stern. He was afraid of the words in his own mouth.

"You know our family keeps its promises," he said. "You know what honor means."

I looked at my mother. She stared at the floor as if the tiles could rescue her.

"What promise?" I asked.
