Then my father's voice came from the sitting room.

"Zahra, come here for a moment."

My pencil stopped moving. I adjusted my glasses and answered quickly, because in our house, nobody ignored Mallam Ibrahim. Not even by accident. He was not a loud man, but his silence carried rules. I still had a scar on my thigh from the year I was nine and thought playing outside was more important than answering him.

I marked my page and walked to my parents' room.
